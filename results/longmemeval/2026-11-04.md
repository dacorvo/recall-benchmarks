# longmemeval, 2026-11-04

funes and seven other tools that search past agent sessions, asked the 470 answerable questions of
LongMemEval over the 19,195 distinct sessions of its LongMemEval-S haystacks, all in one pile.

- machine: AMD EPYC 7R32 (64 threads), 249 GB, Ubuntu 24.04, running several tools at once
- questions: [`longmemeval/questions.jsonl`](../../longmemeval/questions.jsonl), sha256
  `ff93d742ffddb93dcd726f9b5cf8f8b3eddfddbece9655a65aca17a0c233a569`, written by
  `longmemeval/questions.py` from `longmemeval_s_cleaned.json` of `xiaowu0162/longmemeval-cleaned` at
  `98d7416c`. A gold span is an evidence turn and the turn after it, in its session or a copy of it
  ([longmemeval/](../../longmemeval/) says why).
- memory: `dacorvo/funes-xiaowu0162-longmemeval-cleaned-s` at `58450d98655f3d104359fb119933d7a4d6597d13`
- transcripts: the memory's 19,195 sessions and 199,499 turns, rebuilt by the funes recall
  benchmark's `rebuild_sessions.py` at `59b1ed8`, digest
  `3298cc7cdccadb42255aa628d49a963ae7367619642b1f216abd82d3287db7e0`
- every tool ran in its own HOME as the [rules](../../README.md#rules) say. Each passage was placed by
  exact metadata except deja-vu's, which are placed by their text (see deja-vu below).

## funes

funes reads the published memory, so its hits are in the memory's numbering.

- `drivers/funes.py` asked the questions through `funes recall` at its defaults, as an agent does:
  [funes/recall.jsonl](funes/recall.jsonl). It read a local copy of the memory at the same revision,
  at a path no session mentions, so that a hit's `→ get` line is told from one quoted inside another
  hit's text. It returned 8 hits for every question, and funes's rows come from these hits.
- `score_recall` (funes's `benchmark/score_recall.rs`) asked them earlier, scored on the marked spans
  (the questions file before copies). Its output is
  [funes/score_recall-marked.txt](funes/score_recall-marked.txt):


  Its median latency comes from a machine running other tools at the same time.
- On the marked spans, the `funes recall` pass ranks 446 of 470 questions as `score_recall` does.
  Each of the 24 others has a copy of a marked session among its spans. Two of them, asked three
  times each, show why: the copy's turn and the marked one get the same score, and they swap places
  from one run to the next.
- funes and `score_recall` were built from `huggingface/funes` at `29075cb`.

## MemPalace 3.11.0

- `mempalace --palace P mine ~/.claude/projects/ --mode convos`, the backfill MemPalace documents:
  296,084 drawers from 18,468 files. It skips a conversation whose text it has already stored, so
  each of the other 727 files, a copy of a session it kept, is registered without a drawer. The
  `mcp-sweep` arm copies that palace and adds `mempalace sweep ~/.claude/projects/`, one drawer per
  record (199,499), copies included.
- Asked through `mempalace-mcp --palace P`, `mempalace_search {"query": q, "limit": 8}` (its default
  limit is 5), other arguments at their defaults. Its default `minilm` embedder ran on the CPU. Its
  server passed 22 long questions through its query sanitizer, which shortened 6 of them (one from 355
  characters to 83).
- Placement: the driver replays MemPalace's own normalizer and chunker on each file. Its transcript
  equals `normalize_conversations` for 18,416 of the 18,468 files. The 52 others are the sessions that
  hold only assistant turns: none holds a span, and no passage came from them. 295,940 of 295,940
  chunks of the 18,416 files equal their stored drawer. A drawer is the run of turns its text covers:
  median 2 turns per passage returned in `mcp`, at most 4. All 3,760 passages of each arm were placed
  this way.

## agentmemory 0.9.31

- Keyless (no LLM provider, no embeddings), with the iii engine v0.25.0 its CLI pins.
  `agentmemory import-jsonl` reads 200 files by default, 1,000 at most with `--max-files`, and only
  under `~/.claude/projects`. So the driver moved the files into 20 subdirectories there, of up to
  1,000 files each, and imported each with `--max-files 1000`: 19,195 sessions, 199,499 observations,
  one per content block.
- Asked through `agentmemory mcp`: `memory_smart_search` and `memory_recall`, at their default 10
  results. Both rank with BM25 alone when keyless and gave the same hits for every question.
- Placement: an observation keeps its record's timestamp, which places it at that turn. Its id, a
  hash of the record's uuid and the block's index, names the same turn for 4,700 of 4,700 passages.

## deja-vu 0.23.5

- `deja index --rebuild`: it reads 19,195 sessions, 199,499 messages.
- `search`: `deja search --json --limit 8 <q>`. A passage is one of a hit's snippets, found among the
  hit's messages by its text and placed at that message's time (7,037 passages), else by its text in
  the session (44, and 3 of those in several turns, placed at the first). One was not found and counts
  for the session row only.
- `recall`: `deja recall <q>` with `DEJA_RECALL_GLOBAL=1`, run outside any project of the corpus. Its
  page shows 2 to 5 sessions with excerpts, each placed by its text in the session the page names
  (4,486 at one turn, 49 at several, 52 not found and counted for the session row only).
- deja-vu scores itself by sessions on LongMemEval, each question over its own haystack, on its
  [day-zero page](https://vshulcz.github.io/deja-vu/guide/day-zero.html). Over the whole pile its
  session row is 280 (`search`).

## ctx 2.3.0

- `ctx setup --wait`, ctx's documented way of reading existing history, did not finish on this pile:
  its probe of `~/.claude/projects` "hit its scan budget". The driver then ran ctx's import of that
  source, `ctx import --provider claude --path ~/.claude/projects`: 19,195 sessions, 199,499 events.
  Analytics and upgrade checks off.
- `search`: `ctx search --format json -- <q>` at its defaults (lexical, 20 results, one event per
  session). ctx refuses a query of more than 32 distinct analyzed tokens, so 29 questions get no
  result. `ctx search -- <q>` lists the same events in the same order for 470 of 470.
- Placement: a result carries its record's timestamp to the millisecond (8,820 of 8,820 placed by it)
  and the record's line in `event_seq`, which agrees for all of them.

## cass 0.10.0

- `cass index --full --json`: 19,195 conversations, 199,499 messages. `cass search <q> --robot --limit
  8` (its default limit returns every match). A hit carries its message's own timestamp, which places
  it at a turn (every passage of every arm).
- Its lexical query needs every word of the question in one message: 377 of 470 questions get no hit,
  362 without stop words. `hybrid` is the same command after `cass models install --yes` and `cass
  index --semantic` (386,899 MiniLM chunks of the 19,195 conversations), when its default mode fuses
  lexical and semantic results.

## claude-history 0.1.81

- `claude-history agent search <q>`, the interface its Claude Code skill gives an agent, run from a
  directory that is no project of the corpus. Its first search builds the keyword store. `agent read`
  lists 198,065 messages in the 19,195 sessions. The 52 sessions that hold only assistant turns list
  none, and every search warns that they are skipped (none holds a span).
- `--generate-semantic-cache` stops: "semantic core has 150941 unique passages, exceeding the
  50000-entry cache capacity". No option or variable of 0.1.81 sets that capacity, so its `semantic`
  and `hybrid` modes, and their `-nobudget` arms, are not scored.
- Its lexical mode needs every word in a conversation: 397 of 470 questions get no hit as asked, 231
  without stop words. Its 6,000-character output budget cut the end of 140 `keywords` answers, so
  `keywords-nobudget` asks again with `--no-budget`.
- Placement: a hit names message ordinals (`focus=mA..mB`), placed at the file lines `agent read`
  gives for them. Every hit here is one message.

## agentsview 0.46.1

- `AGENTSVIEW_NO_DAEMON=1 agentsview sync`: 19,195 sessions, 199,499 messages. Searches go through its
  daemon, stopped at the end. Telemetry and update checks off.
- `default`: `agentsview session search <q> --json`, a substring match: 469 of the 470 questions
  appear nowhere as typed. `fts` adds `--fts`, which needs every term in one message: 446 get nothing.
- Its default session list counts 17,884 of the 19,195 sessions (counting every kind of session
  outlasts the daemon's 30-second response timeout). Search leaves out one-shot sessions by default:
  `fts-keywords-oneshot` adds `--include-one-shot` and finds the same 25.
- Placement: each match carries its message's timestamp, which places it (every passage).
